Your 30 / 60 / 90 Day Credit Plan

These steps can help you develop healthier credit habits. Results vary based on your full credit profile.

First 30 Days

  • Pay every account by its due date.
  • Review all credit-card balances and limits.
  • Work toward lowering high utilization.
  • Avoid unnecessary new credit applications.
  • Review your credit reports for errors.

Days 31–60

  • Continue making every payment on time.
  • Make another payment toward revolving balances.
  • Keep older accounts open when appropriate.
  • Track reported balances each month.
  • Dispute inaccurate credit-report information directly with the appropriate bureau.

Days 61–90

  • Check whether utilization has decreased.
  • Keep balances manageable before statements close.
  • Continue building positive payment history.
  • Review your progress and update your payoff goal.
  • Repeat the plan instead of chasing quick-score promises.

Credit Builder FAQ

What is credit utilization?

Credit utilization compares revolving credit balances with available revolving credit limits. For example, a $300 balance on a $1,000 limit equals 30% utilization.

Will getting below 30% guarantee my score increases?

No. Credit scores depend on many factors and different scoring models. Lower utilization may help, but no particular score increase is guaranteed.

Can paying rent build credit?

Certain services can report eligible rent payments to participating credit bureaus. Whether that affects a particular score depends on the person's credit file and the scoring model being used.

Does using this calculator change my credit score?

No. This calculator does not access or alter your credit report. It helps you estimate utilization and plan potential next steps.
