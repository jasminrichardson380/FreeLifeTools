How the Rent Affordability Calculator Works

The calculator first looks at your monthly take-home income. It then compares a 30% income guideline with the amount of money remaining after the non-housing expenses you entered.

The planning estimate uses the lower of the two amounts. This is intended to give you a starting point for comparing rental prices with your overall monthly budget.

Example

Monthly take-home income: $3,200

30% of monthly income: $960

If regular non-housing expenses total $1,500, you would have $1,700 remaining before rent.

In this example, the calculator would use $960 as the more conservative planning estimate.

What Is the 30% Rent Guideline?

A commonly referenced housing guideline uses about 30% of income for housing costs. It can be useful as a quick starting point, but it does not determine what every household can comfortably afford.

Two people with the same income can have very different budgets. Debt payments, transportation, childcare, insurance, groceries and savings goals can all affect how much money is realistically available for housing.

Take-home income vs. gross income

This FreeLifeTools calculator uses the take-home income you enter because it is designed as a personal budgeting tool. A landlord may instead evaluate your gross income before taxes and deductions.

Expenses to Consider Before Renting

Rent is only one part of the cost of living in a home or apartment. When comparing properties, consider other costs that may affect your monthly budget.

  • Electricity
  • Water and sewer
  • Internet
  • Renter's insurance
  • Parking fees
  • Pet fees
  • Transportation costs
  • Application fees
  • Security deposits
  • Moving expenses

Some rental properties include certain utilities while others require tenants to pay them separately, so it can help to ask about these costs before signing a lease.

Rent Affordability FAQ

How much rent can I afford?

There is no single amount that works for everyone. Your income, debt, transportation costs, childcare, utilities, savings and other expenses can all affect how much rent fits your budget.

Does a landlord use take-home income?

Rental qualification rules vary. Some landlords and property managers use gross income rather than take-home pay when reviewing an application.

Does the calculator guarantee that I will qualify for an apartment?

No. The calculator is a budgeting tool only. Rental qualification requirements are determined by the landlord or property manager.

Should utilities be included in my rent budget?

Yes, it can be helpful to consider utilities and other housing-related costs when deciding whether a rental fits your overall monthly budget.

Is this rent calculator free?

Yes. The FreeLifeTools Rent Affordability Calculator can be used for free.
